*In progress. Sits under the larger thought: at the root of suffering is desire, and human nature is to desire.*

One can draw a fascinating distinction concerning feelings of jealousy and envy. Because people use the words ambiguously, don't worry about how they match conventional usage; consider instead the beauty of the definition. Let envy be of two kinds:

**Spoiling envy** carries the impulse to spoil the thing that we are envious of another having. For example, if someone has a lovely coat that we are envious of, we might have an idea and impulse to put out a cigarette on it, or spill wine over it, so as to spoil the coat.

**Killing envy** carries the impulse to make the thing we are envious of not exist. Someone on stage singing beautifully, we may wish to cut the rope to the curtain and have them simply disappear behind it — to end our envy.

**Jealousy** is founded in repressed desire. Jealousy can transform into an understanding of our desire — we don't usually want exactly what we are jealous of, but there is some something that we do want, and some way we restrain ourselves. If we see the restraint, we can see the desire, and then work toward our desire.
