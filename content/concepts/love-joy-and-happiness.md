Consider defining that 'you Truly love someone' as:

1. You not only have the desire to make the person 'happy', but also the capacity to do so.
2. You not only have the desire to ease the person's suffering, but also the capacity to do so.
3. Out of your relationship arises a sense of 'joy'.
4. Out of your relationship arises a sense of freedom.

And, what's the difference between joy and happiness? In this definition, joy and happiness are used to distinguish two underlying fundamentally different concepts. Imagine you have a delicious looking meal. It's so delicious looking that you feel a happy joyous excitement at the idea of how good it is going to be to eat it. This is the concept of 'joy' being referred to above. And then, when you eat it, you find it so delicious, and that experience is the 'happy' being referred to above. This distinguishes anticipatory 'joy' from the experience of liking the present sensations: 'happiness'. Two feelings of liking with different natures.

Of course, in everyday usage, happiness and joy are used in many other ways too. But it's interesting to think about love consisting of both the luscious anticipatory aspect, as well as the immediate full bodied sensory one.
