The auto-play function of YouTube accidentally led me to a TED talk on the sidebar titled "Marry the real person you should marry" by a Hollywood television writer who seems narcissistic. As you may guess, the "big" idea is that we should marry ourselves. TED talks, as the Internet fast foods, have disappointed me over the years; something about the TED platform has turned much of its so-called ideas into spiritual malaise as opposed to inspiration. Maybe this is just a bias. I let the idea of marrying yourself entertain nevertheless — it might be a good avenue for thinking about broader things.

According to the speaker (who perceives marriage as the royal path to secure herself, as she often felt left alone growing up with a criminal father and a prostitute mother), she realizes after 3 divorces that the only relationship we can have with another person is the relationship we are already having with ourselves. The implications are romantic — that we take a vow to ourselves: no matter whether I am poor or rich, in a bad relationship or not, weighing 10 pounds more or less, having a good or bad hair day, I take a vow to love myself. I take a vow to carry myself over the hard waters, I stay for the "for worse" parts of the for better or worse.

The context in which I mentioned this idea in therapy was when I felt conflicted by the inner dialogue, the practice of psychoanalysis, where we constantly retune the nuances of how we relate to ourselves. I burst out saying, "it is as if I am married to myself, and it is such a big world in there, between us, and it's exhausting!". So yes, my psychoanalysis is much like the process of a marriage with myself.
