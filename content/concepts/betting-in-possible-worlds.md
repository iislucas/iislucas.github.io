Here's a riddle in the form of a game: There are two boxes, in one you will find twice as much as in the other. You may pick one, and when you do, you may look at the amount. And then, I will offer you a choice: you may change your choice of box, for a very small fee, or you may stick with your choice. You play...

In the first box you find $10, and I say you may change for $0.50. Should you change?

**Rationale (1)** Well, imagine that under one box is X amount, under the other 2X. And you chose randomly, so you are equally likely to have the larger amount as the smaller amount. So, you reason, if you pay, you'll be equally likely to end up with the smaller amount as the larger — but you'll be $0.50 poorer. So you'd best just stick with your first choice. Sounds rational, right?

**Rationale (2)** But, let us think about it another way... you picked $10, if this is the larger sum (there was a 50% chance you picked the larger sum), then the other box contains $5, and you'll be left with $4.50. On the other hand, perhaps you picked the box with the smaller sum (also a 50% chance). If so, then changing will get you $20, and after the $0.50 changing fee, you'll have $19.50. So the average expected amount you'll get after changing will be ($4.50 + $19.50) / 2 = $12. That's $2 more than staying with $10, so you should change for sure! Sounds rational, right?

Both logics are correct. The paradox is that the question didn't tell you everything you needed to know! There are two possible worlds. The problem description elided a key bit of information: how did the money get there exactly? Was the amount in both boxes determined before you made your first choice of box (rationale 1), or was the amount in the second box determined after you chose the first, according to the rule (rationale 2)? Our mind jumps to one definition or the other, but both satisfy the description that was given!
